                  Sick leave (лікарняний) calculation rules — Ukraine

                   1. Calculation period
                       Last 12 calendar months before the month of illness. Use earnings on which USC
                       (ЄСВ) was paid.

                   2. Denominator
                       Not calendar days of the month — days the employee was actually insured (working,

https://youtrack.hrblizz.dev/tickets/HRBS-14331                                                               7/15
9/23/26, 9:30 AM                                 Ukraine WTC catalog Altium client check : HRBS-14331

                  with USC paid). Public holidays are ALWAYS excluded here (this is a general rule, not
                  just during martial law).

                  3. What's removed from the calculation period
                     Only periods with no substitute earnings at all:

                  prior sick leave
                  maternity leave (pregnancy/childbirth)
                  childcare leave up to age 3
                  unpaid leave
                  suspension of the employment contract due to war
                  military service, where average earnings were not retained

                  (Unlike vacation pay — here sick leave and maternity leave themselves are removed,
                  otherwise you'd be calculating "sick pay from sick pay.")

                  4. Short work history

                  1–12 months of service → only fully worked months
                  less than a month → actual calendar days
                  fell ill on the very first working day → contractual rate / 30.44

                  5. Payout percentage — depends on total insurance record (stazh)

                  under 3 years → 50%
                  3–5 years → 60%
                  5–8 years → 70%
                  over 8 years → 100%

                  Exception: combat veterans and certain other privileged categories → always 100%, stazh
                  doesn't matter.

                  Maternity benefit (pregnancy/childbirth) — always 100%, stazh has no effect on the
                  percentage at all.

                  6. Stazh under 6 months in the last 12
                     The stazh percentage tiers don't apply here at all. Instead, the amount is simply
                     capped: no more than minimum wage / 30.44 per day.

                  7. Who pays

                  ordinary illness — days 1–5 employer, from day 6 onward the Pension Fund (funds the
                  employer via a заява-розрахунок claim; the employer itself makes the actual payment to
                  the employee)
                  maternity leave — Pension Fund from day 1
                  caring for a sick child or family member — Pension Fund from day 1 (the employer doesn't
                  pay anything, not even the first days)

                  8. Bonuses in the sick pay calculation — simpler than for vacation pay
                     Monthly bonus — only counted if accrued for a month that falls within the calculation
                     period. Quarterly/annual bonuses — included in the month they're accrued, with no
                     spreading across months (unlike vacation pay).

                  9. Taxation
                     Ordinary sick leave: 18% PDFO + 5% military levy + 22% USC (8.41% for disability) —
                     on the full amount, both the employer-paid days and the Pension-Fund-funded days.
                     Maternity benefit (декретні): exempt from PDFO and military levy entirely (Art. 165.1.1
                     of the Tax Code — it's a state social insurance payment, not wage income). USC (22%
                     / 8.41%) still applies — that part isn't exempt, only PDFO and the levy are.

Codes concerned: 62570 (Per Diem Allowance), 65570 / 65571 / 65572 (Per diem benefit
— domestic), 65580 / 65581 / 65582 (Per diem benefit — foreign), and 62965 (Unreturned
accountable excess). All of these are substantively the same type of payment and
currently carry identical settings: 4DF income code 118, PIT = Yes, Military Levy = Yes,
ESV = No.

What these wage types are under Ukrainian law. Per diem ("добові") is a reimbursement of
the employee's subsistence costs during a business trip, not remuneration for work. It is
not part of the payroll fund (п. 3.15 of the Instruction on Labour Statistics, approved by
State Statistics Service Order No. 5 of 13.01.2004). Consequently it is outside the ESV
base and is excluded from every average-earnings calculation — vacation pay, sick leave
and the two-month base for business trips and donor days. Salary for the days of the
business trip is a separate wage type.

Taxation depends on the amount, so PIT and Military Levy cannot be set to a flat Yes.
Three scenarios apply under пп. 170.9.1 of the Tax Code of Ukraine:

Within the statutory cap — no PIT, no Military Levy, no ESV. For 2026 the cap is UAH
864.70 per calendar day for domestic trips (0.1 of the minimum wage established as at 1
January of the reporting year: 8,647 × 0.1) and EUR 80 per calendar day for foreign trips,
converted at the official NBU rate set for each such day.
Above the cap — the excess only is included in the employee's taxable income and is
subject to PIT and Military Levy, with the gross-up coefficient of 1.219512 applied to the
PIT base. ESV is still not charged, as this remains an employer expense rather than salary.
Business trip not evidenced by documents — the full per diem amount becomes taxable,
not just the excess.

What we ask to review. For all the codes listed above, the income code 118 and ESV = No
are correct and should stay. The PIT and Military Levy flags need to be aligned with the
intended use of each code. The cleanest configuration is to split the amount into two
wage types: one for per diem within the statutory cap (income code 118, no PIT, no
Military Levy, no ESV) and one for the excess over the cap (income code 118, PIT and
Military Levy with the 1.219512 gross-up, no ESV). If instead a single code is kept with the
cap applied inside the formula, the formula must hold both the daily cap (domestic and
foreign) and the gross-up coefficient — the existing rate and unit codes 63711, 63712,
75100 and 75101 appear to be intended for exactly that, so please confirm how they
interact with 62570.

Code 62965 (Unreturned accountable excess) follows the same gross-up logic but on a
different legal ground: it covers accountable funds not returned within the statutory
deadline, which are taxable in full under пп. 170.9.1. Its PIT and Military Levy flags are
therefore correct as Yes; only the gross-up treatment needs confirming.

The current combination is not valid under Ukrainian law. Income code 101 means the
payment is qualified as salary, and salary is by definition subject to ESV. A wage type
cannot be reported as salary and be exempt from ESV at the same time. The correct
setting depends entirely on one question: is the Ukrainian legal entity the source of this
income?

Scenario A — the Ukrainian entity grants the shares, or the group programme is recharged
to it and it recognises the award as income of its own employee. Only in this case may
these wage types be used in Ukrainian payroll. The award is then a payment within the
employment relationship: under п. 2.3.3 of the Instruction on Labour Statistics (State
Statistics Committee Order No. 5 of 13.01.2004) other incentive and compensatory
payments form part of the payroll fund, and shares granted as a form of bonus or
corporate reward are a payroll-fund component. Consequently:

the income is qualified as salary and reported in Annex 4DF under income code 101;
as the income is in non-monetary form, the PIT base is grossed up under п. 164.5 of the
Tax Code, coefficient 1.219512;
Military Levy is charged at 5% (on the nominal amount, without the gross-up);
ESV is charged under part 1 of Article 7 of Law No. 2464-VI, because this is a payment
within the employment relationship. The monthly maximum ESV base applies — UAH
172,940 in 2026 (20 × the minimum wage of UAH 8,647), counted together with the
employee's other payroll income of the month;
the average-earnings columns must be revisited: the sick-leave column becomes Yes (the
base for sickness benefit is all ESV-liable income), while the vacation and business-trip
columns depend on whether the award is systematic or one-off — a one-off award is
excluded by sub-paras (b) and (i) of п. 4 of Resolution No. 100.

Scenario B — the shares are granted directly by the foreign parent company. If the
Ukrainian entity did not transfer the shares, did not bear or reimburse their cost and is not
acting as part of the group reward programme, it is not the source of the income and
therefore not a tax agent (п. 171.1 of the Tax Code). The award is then foreign income of
the individual under пп. 163.1.3, self-declared in the annual tax return under пп. 168.2.1 and
Article 179: the employee personally pays PIT at 18% and Military Levy at 5%, and the
gross-up coefficient does not apply, as it is applied only by a tax agent. In this scenario no
ESV arises, nothing is withheld by the employer, and the payment must not be processed
through these wage types at all — it does not appear in payroll or in the 4DF annex.
