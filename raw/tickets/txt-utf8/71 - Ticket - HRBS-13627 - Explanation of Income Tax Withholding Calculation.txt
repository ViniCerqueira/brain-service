The system should automatically calculate income tax for all employees, as applicable, using
either Method 1 or Method 2 with a fixed percentage, Concepts Used for Income Tax Withholding
The following concepts are used for income tax withholding:
• 2552 – Income Tax (Employee): Used for the general employee population.
• 21330 – Personal Income Tax Withholding (Theoretical): Used for employees classified as Expat
Home.
For the monthly payroll process, the income tax withholding calculation is initially performed
using the standard methodology for all employees. Once the calculation is completed, the values
corresponding to employees under the Expat Home treatment are reviewed and adjusted in the
system according to the applicable methodology.
Additionally, the withholding tax amount is reclassified to the corresponding concept according
to the employee's type or classification, ensuring that the value is recorded under the
appropriate concept.
The validation file is attached to provide further detail and facilitate understanding of each item.
The file corresponds to a monthly Excel-based validator, with worksheets for the standard
calculation (“Tax Report” and “Gross to Net PMI”), contingent withholding calculation
(“Contingente”), special calculation for the service bonus (“ReteFuente PRIMA”), tax rate table
(“Tax Table”), deduction inputs (“Tax Ded”), and the recalculation of the fixed semiannual
withholding percentage (“Recaculo Retencio”).
The analysis focuses on identifying the legal thresholds and limits automatically applied by the
file, verifying that they correspond to the regulations applicable for the period (July 2026), and
explaining step by step the calculation logic followed by each formula.

Tax Value Unit (UVT) Used
The file uses a UVT value of $52,374 in all threshold formulas (worksheets “Gross to Net PMI”
and “Tax Table”).
This value corresponds to the official UVT for tax year 2026, established by the Colombian Tax
Authority (DIAN) through Resolution 000238 dated December 15, 2025, based on the 5.17%
variation in the CPI for middle-income households between October 2024 and October 2025.
Regulatory Framework
• Article 383 of the Colombian Tax Code (E.T.): Marginal tax rate table for withholding tax on
employment income.
• Article 386 E.T.: Procedure 2 (fixed semiannual withholding percentage).
• Article 387 E.T.: Deductions for housing loan interest, health/medical insurance, and
dependents.
• Article 387-1 E.T.: Treatment of meal allowance or subsidy.
• Article 388 E.T.: 40% limitation applicable to deductions and exempt income in the taxable base
calculation.
• Article 206, numeral 10 E.T.: 25% exempt income applicable to employment payments.
• Article 126-1 E.T.: 30% limitation applicable to voluntary contributions to AFC and voluntary
pension funds.

Detailed Explanation of the 40% Limitation – Article 388 E.T.
This is one of the most sensitive controls because it combines two different limits, and the more
restrictive limit must be applied:
• Relative limit: The sum of deductions (housing, prepaid medical plan and dependents),
AFC/VFP contributions, and the 25% exempt income cannot exceed 40% of Base 1 (income less
mandatory social security contributions).
• Absolute limit: Regardless of the result above, the total benefit cannot exceed 1,340 UVT per
year, equivalent to approximately 111.67 UVT per month ($5,847,117 based on the 2026 UVT).
• Rule applied: The file calculates MIN(40% of Base 1, actual sum of benefits) and then applies a
second MIN against the absolute annual-limit monthly equivalent. The resulting benefit is
deducted from Base 1 to determine the final taxable base.
Special Case: Employees Who Have Already Reached the 1,340 UVT Annual Limit
The 1,340 UVT limit under Article 388 E.T. is, by definition, an annual limit. No employee may
receive deductions and exempt income exceeding 1,340 UVT in total during the 12 months of the
tax year (approximately $70,181,160 for 2026).
The validator does not apply this limit based on an accumulated annual amount. Instead, each
month it uses cell $V$11 = (1,340 × UVT) ÷ 12 ≈ 111.67 UVT/month as a proportional monthly
equivalent and applies it consistently from January through December.
In general cases, this approach is mathematically consistent: if the 111.67 UVT monthly limit is
strictly respected in each of the 12 months, the maximum annual amount is limited to 12 × 111.67
UVT = 1,340 UVT.
However, for an employee whose actual accumulated deductions and exempt income have
already reached 1,340 UVT during the year, no additional benefit should be applied during the
remaining months of the same tax year, even if the monthly formula appears to allow another
deduction.
Applying only the monthly limit without checking the actual annual accumulated amount may
result in withholding below the applicable amount (under-withholding), generating a difference
that could ultimately be payable by the employee when filing the annual income tax return or
requiring an adjustment by the company in subsequent months.
For these employees, a manual control is maintained for those who received this benefit through
the payment of IC bonuses, as the client requested that the benefit be applied in full to reduce
the withholding tax amount for the month.

Special Treatment of the Service Bonus – Procedure 1
The “ReteFuente PRIMA” worksheet documents—and the validator applies—the DIAN criterion
according to which the service bonus is not subject to the general tax withholding calculation.
Mandatory social security contributions, Article 387 E.T. deductions, and the 40% limitation are
not deducted from this payment.
The only amount that may be deducted is the 25% exempt income under Article 206, numeral 10
E.T., as this is exempt income rather than a deduction.
The applicable marginal tax rate from the Article 383 E.T. table is then applied directly to the
resulting net amount.
For Procedure 2, the service bonus is added to the employee's taxable income for the month.
Clarification Regarding Withholding Tax Configuration for Expatriate/Non-Resident Employees
For employees identified by the client under this treatment, withholding tax is configured based
on the total taxable earnings for the period, to which a 20% tax rate is applied.
Under this configuration, tax benefits, deductions, and exempt income are not considered.
Therefore, the calculation corresponds to applying the 20% rate directly to the total taxable
earnings.
The client is directly responsible for monitoring the employees' tax status and supporting
documentation and for identifying which employees should be treated under this methodology.
The Payroll team applies the configuration based on the information and instructions provided by
the client.
If the client determines that an employee should move to a different tax withholding calculation
method, the request must be submitted in writing, together with the corresponding instructions.
Once the written request is received, the employee's withholding tax calculation is updated and
processed under Procedure 1, applying the applicable deductions, exempt income, and other tax-
related items based on the employee's information and supporting documentation.
Therefore, any change to the calculation methodology must be supported by written
confirmation from the client and the corresponding documentation, based on which the payroll
configuration will be updated.
Contingent Withholding
The “Contingente” worksheet replicates the same calculation structure used for Procedure 1, but
applies it to a base that incorporates additional or non-recurring payments during the period,
such as bonuses, adjustments, retroactive payments, etc.
The purpose of this parallel calculation is to project the marginal impact that the additional
income would have on the applicable withholding tax rate, in order to prevent under-withholding
when the additional payment moves the employee into a higher tax bracket.
The validator compares the “with contingency” result against the regular calculation and uses the
positive difference as the additional withholding tax to be applied (column “CONTINGENT
WITHHOLDING” in “Tax Report”).
