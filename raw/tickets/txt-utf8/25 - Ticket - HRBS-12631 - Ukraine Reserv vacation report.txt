Dear @lily

In Ukraine, tracking the vacation reserve is also a statutory requirement at the state level —
specifically: the entitled number of vacation days, the number of days already taken, and the
remaining balance, calculated proportionally to time actually worked. This is internal report.

With that in mind, please consider adding automatic vacation reserve calculation per employee in
HRBlizz.

Could we add the following fields/attributes to the Data Dictionary:

Vacation days remaining – calculated automatically
Vacation days used – calculated automatically from payroll
Entitled vacation days – a field applicable to all employees

For example: the standard entitlement is 24 days per year, counted from the hire date (i.e., from
the hire date to the same date the following year, the employee is entitled to 24 days). Each
month, vacation should accrue proportionally to time worked. When an employee is terminated,
this data would let us pull the exact number of vacation days owed — or identify any excess days
taken (overuse).

We would also need an exportable vacation report showing, for a given period, how many days
were taken and how many days remain.

Example layout to be uploaded in Excel.

                  Hi @Wallisson dos Santos Gomes please check this 3 points in the your file of Ukraine
                  guid:

                  1. "3-banking-day rule" is overstated as universal. It only applies to cash disbursements
                     or non-monetary income. For bank-transfer salaries (the vast majority of cases),
                     PIT/Military Levy must be paid on the same day as the salary transfer — banks won't
                     process the payroll payment without a simultaneous tax payment order. So "3 banking
                     days" is the exception (cash), not the general rule.

                  2. The 53-week year section is a hypothetical engineering construct, not a documented
                     legal rule. Weekly payroll periods aren't standard practice under Ukrainian law (the
                     norm is monthly accounting with payment at least twice a month, Labour Code Art.
                     115). The logic itself is internally consistent, but it should be labeled as "how this
                     would theoretically work," not as an established rule.
