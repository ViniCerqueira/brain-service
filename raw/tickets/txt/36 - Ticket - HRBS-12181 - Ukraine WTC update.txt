@Lily Li

For this requirement I need to understand the system behavior, like the non-taxable
amount -- the system will exclude it from the taxable wage type, right? We do not need
to give a separate code, am I correct here? And for the taxable wage code, 4 series
126 -- so for the taxable benefit I can use the 126 4 series.

The non-taxable part goes under code "160" and is treated as personified income of a
specific individual (tied to a named person's tax ID).

The taxable excess (the amount above the 25% threshold) is not reported under "160."
Instead it is taxed as an additional benefit ( ) and reported under a
different income code, typically "126."

So the "series" you're referring to isn't a range of codes -- "160" is a single distinct income
code in the 4 classification, and it pairs with code "126" when a gift exceeds the limit
and part becomes taxable.

                 Nataliia Hahan Commented 21 days ago
                 @Wallisson dos Santos Gomes

                 Hi Wallisson,

                 Thanks for the breakdown. One correction to the scope, based on PKU:

                  1. Not all 126 codes should carry the coefficient.
                      Per p. 164.5 PKU (and pp. 164.2.17 PKU), the 1.219512 coefficient applies ONLY
                      when the benefit is provided in non-monetary form (in-kind: property, services, use of
                      company assets, etc.). If the BIK is paid in cash, no coefficient applies -- PDFO 18% is
                      calculated straight on the amount, no gross-up.
                      So the catalog shouldn't apply the coefficient to all 109 codes uniformly -- only to the
                      non-monetary ones.

                  2. Not everything with 4DF=126 should even be 126.
                      Per pp. 14.1.47 PKU, additional benefit is income NOT tied to the employer's
                      obligation under the employment relationship. If a cash payment is structured in the
                      contract as an element of pay (part of the remuneration system -- e.g. "",
                      "", not " "), it's wage income (101), not BIK -- regardless
                      of it being paid in monetary or fixed monthly.

                 Proposal:

                      Keep a small set of codes as 126 WITHOUT the coefficient -- for monetary-form
                      benefits that are genuinely BIK (not wage) and paid as monetary benefit.
                      Keep the coefficient only on the non-monetary 126 codes.
                      Other benefits -- as 101 (Other/Additional Salary) and we can rename accordingly
                      when we will need it
