                  Heidy Ballesteros Puyo  Commented about 1 month ago
                  Hi Romano!

                  1. For court-ordered garnishments:

                  For court-ordered garnishments, the system should automatically calculate one-fifth (1/5)
                  of the amount exceeding the SMMLV, based on the following calculation:

                  1. Start with the applicable salary earnings, according to the court order.
                  2. Deduct the following employee deductions:

                          2552 - Income Tax (employee)
                          2553 - Solidarity Pension Fund
                          2554 - Medical Plan - Healthcare Insurance (EPS) Employee
                          2556 - Pension Plan Mandatory - AFP Employee
                  3. From the resulting amount, deduct the current SMMLV of $1,750,905.
                  4. Calculate one-fifth (1/5) of the resulting amount.

                  This calculation must be performed automatically by the system, as we will not have
                  access to manually modify the garnishment amount before payroll is generated.

                  It is important to consider that the calculation depends on the specific court order. There
                  are different scenarios:

                  Garnishment based on salary earnings: In some cases, the court order establishes
                  that the garnishment applies to the employee's salary earnings. In this case, the
                  system should automatically calculate the amount according to the methodology
                  described above.

                  Garnishment based only on basic salary: In other cases, the court order specifies that
                  the garnishment applies only to the employee's basic salary. In this case, the system
                  should also calculate the garnishment automatically based on the basic salary,
                  applying the corresponding deductions, the SMMLV threshold, and the one-fifth rule.

                  In both scenarios, the calculated amount cannot be manually modified before payroll is
                  generated.

                  Court-ordered fixed amount: There are also cases where the judge specifies a fixed
                  garnishment amount. In this scenario, there should be no calculation. The system
                  should simply apply the single fixed amount entered in the employee's garnishment
                  record.

                  Additionally, there may be cases where no garnishment applies because the resulting
                  calculation base is lower than the current SMMLV. For 2026, the SMMLV is $1,750,905.

                  In these cases, the system must not generate a negative garnishment amount. If the
                  resulting base is equal to or lower than the SMMLV, the garnishment amount should be
                  zero (0).

                  Therefore, the system should automatically determine and calculate the garnishment
                  according to the type of court order registered for the employee, without requiring manual
                  adjustments before payroll generation.
