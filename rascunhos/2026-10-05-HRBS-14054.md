**Reply (English):**

Hi Daniel,

Yes, in principle: a grossed-up car allowance is remuneration paid to the employee, so the grossed-up amount forms part of the gross that feeds the TUS. One condition decides whether all of it is in the TUS base (see "Condition" below).

**Legal basis (as in our Congo research)**

- TUS (Taxe Unique sur les Salaires) is 7.5%, employer only, on total gross, with no ceiling, for all sectors. It is remitted 5.475% on the CNSS bordereau and 2.025% on the DGID declaration, mandatory from 01-01-2026 (loi n°42-2025, Finance Law 2026, as set out in our CCG).
- The TUS follows the ITS perimeter. A cash allowance that is in the ITS taxable base is therefore also in the TUS base. The ITS base is set by loi n°42-2025 Art. 115.

**Condition: the 15% cap on special allowances**

- Forfaitary special allowances are exempt from ITS only up to 15% of total gross salary (before the CNSS employee deduction and before the 20% abatement). Any excess is brought back into the ITS base and, because the TUS follows the ITS perimeter, into the TUS base. The exemption does not apply to CNSS or CAMU.
- If the client treats the car allowance as a special allowance within that cap, that part is outside the ITS and TUS bases, and the gross-up would follow it. If it is paid as ordinary cash remuneration, the whole grossed-up amount is in the TUS base. Please confirm which classification the client uses.

**On the discrepancy in the ticket**

- For the two employees with an empty "Gross up" column, the car allowance sits at the flat 500,000 with no markup. The TUS is only as accurate as the gross that feeds it, so those two will be understated. From the ticket this is a client G2N data gap and not a compliance rule, as already noted by the author. We cannot validate the amounts without the attachment.
- Our research does not describe the mechanics of a net-guaranteed allowance (how the gross-up itself is calculated). We confirm the base, not the formula.

**Impact on artifacts**

- CCG: no change; it already states the TUS base (total gross, no ceiling) and the 15% special-allowance rule.
- WTC: the car allowance wage type must be flagged for TUS the same as for ITS. The grossed-up component needs the same flags as the allowance it grosses up. We have not seen the client's wage type for it.

**What we need from the client**

- Whether the car allowance is treated as a forfaitary special allowance (15% cap) or as ordinary cash remuneration.
- The grade-based net amounts and whether they are in XAF.
